## Why This Exists

Most people grind DSA to pass interviews. That's the wrong target.

I'm building this to develop the kind of engineering judgment that top companies actually pay for — the ability to look at an unfamiliar, messy problem and reason your way to an efficient, correct, defensible solution under pressure. Interviews are just the checkpoint. The real goal is becoming an engineer whose thinking holds up in a system design review, a production incident, or a whiteboard with a bar raiser watching.

Every file in here is proof of that thinking — not just a working answer.

---

## The Standard I Hold Myself To

A solution isn't "done" when it passes the test cases. It's done when it meets all of these:

- **Correct** — proven to work on edge cases, not just the happy path
- **Justified** — I can explain *why* this approach, and why not the alternatives
- **Analyzed** — time and space complexity stated, not guessed
- **Clean** — readable by someone else without a walkthrough
- **Optimized** — pushed past the first working brute-force version

If a solution doesn't hit all five, it's not finished — it's a draft.

---

## How I Approach a Problem

No solution goes straight from "read the question" to "write code." Every problem in this repo goes through the same discipline:

1. **Understand** — restate the problem in my own words, identify constraints, find edge cases before writing a single line
2. **Plan** — think in brute-force first, then ask what's being wasted (time? space? repeated work?) and how to fix it
3. **Implement** — write clean C++, using the right data structure for the job, not the first one that comes to mind
4. **Analyze** — derive Big-O for time and space, explicitly, every time
5. **Reflect** — could this be simpler? faster? does the pattern generalize to other problems?

Step 5 is the one most people skip. It's also the one that actually builds intuition instead of just memorized templates.

## What This Is *Not*

- Not a LeetCode-streak flex
- Not copy-pasted editorial solutions with my name on them
- Not "I solved 500 problems" as a vanity metric
- Not memorized patterns applied blindly without understanding *why* they work

Volume without understanding is wasted effort. I'd rather deeply understand 150 problems than shallowly touch 800.

## Language & Tools

**C++** — chosen deliberately for interview rounds: STL fluency, manual memory/complexity control, and the performance-level thinking it forces that higher-level languages let you skip.

## Where This Is Heading

This repository is one input into a larger goal: becoming a full-stack capable, DSA-sharp, interview-ready software engineer who can walk into a top-tech interview loop and hold their own — technically and in how they communicate their reasoning.

Progress here is continuous, not linear. Some weeks are pure grinding through patterns. Others are revisiting old solutions with a sharper eye and rewriting them because "it worked" isn't good enough anymore.
